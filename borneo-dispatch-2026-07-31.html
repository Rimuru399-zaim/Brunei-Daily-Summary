Kospi Surges 13 Per Cent, Erasing a Week of Losses in a Single Morning

Samsung and SK Hynix lead a violent rebound in chipmaking shares, days after a sell-off over an AI bubble forced the Korea Exchange to halt trading altogether.

Wire Reports · Seoul · New York

South Korea's Kospi index rose more than 13 per cent in early trading on Friday, recovering in a single session most of the ground it had lost during a week that ranks among the most turbulent in the exchange's recent history. The index was quoted at 6,330.59, a gain of 13.1 per cent, with reports of an intraday advance beyond 17 per cent.

The move was concentrated in the chipmakers. Samsung Electronics rose 19.3 per cent and SK Hynix, the memory manufacturer, gained 22.6 per cent, with later quotations placing both higher still. The scale of the advance triggered the Korea Exchange's sidecar mechanism, suspending programme trading for five minutes.

The rebound followed two days of severe losses. The Kospi fell by more than 16 per cent across Tuesday and Wednesday amid a global retreat from technology shares, driven by concern that valuations in artificial intelligence had run ahead of earnings and by intensifying competition from Chinese chipmakers. Tuesday's decline of more than 8 per cent was steep enough to trigger a circuit breaker and halt trading outright.

Wall Street set the tone for Friday's recovery. On Thursday the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 613.92 points, or 1.2 per cent, to close at 52,208.06; the S&P 500 gained 1.7 per cent to 7,437.64; and the Nasdaq Composite added 2.8 per cent to 25,122.18, lifted by a 15.5 per cent advance in Microsoft after its results.

The turn came a day after the Federal Reserve, under Chair Kevin Warsh, voted 9–3 to leave interest rates unchanged — a decision investors initially read as hawkish, sending shares lower on Wednesday before they reversed course.

For the region, the episode is a reminder of how tightly Southeast Asian markets are now coupled to the semiconductor cycle. Malaysian equities held up through the week, with the FBM KLCI described as resilient despite the American sell-off, and the ringgit opened higher after the Fed's hold.

Growth Seen Recovering to 2.3 Per Cent as Energy Prices Lift Revenue

Brunei's economy is projected to expand by 2.3 per cent this year, according to the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office, a marked improvement on the 0.7 per cent recorded in 2025 and a recovery attributed largely to elevated oil and liquefied natural gas prices feeding through to export earnings and fiscal revenue.

Warisan Disowns Poster Calling Supporters to Padang Merdeka
