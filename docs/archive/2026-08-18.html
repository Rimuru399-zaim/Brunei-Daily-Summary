Anwar stresses need to bolster Sabah defence, voices concern over US military expansion in Philippines

Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim has stated that Malaysia requires additional defence spending, potentially ranging from hundreds of millions to billions of ringgit, to secure sufficient assets and capabilities amid rising tensions between the United States and China. Speaking at the closing of the 2027 Budget engagement session in Putrajaya, Anwar emphasised the need to assess national security requirements and bolster defences in Sabah while maintaining good relations with both major powers.

Anwar expressed concern regarding security developments in the southern Philippines, noting that Balabac Island—located 20 minutes from Malaysia's Banggi Island—has been developed by the Philippine government and the United States into a major military base. While acknowledging that Malaysia lacks the resources to construct bases of similar sophistication, Anwar stated that the country's basic defence capabilities in Sabah must be strengthened.

Singapore tightens anti-scam rules on social media, messaging platforms

Singapore has introduced new rules under the Online Criminal Harms Act requiring messaging platforms to restrict unknown contacts and social media services to verify advertisers, following scam losses exceeding US$2.8 billion from 2020 through the first half of 2025. Under the measures, seven designated communication services—WhatsApp, Telegram, WeChat, Apple iMessage, Apple FaceTime, Google Messages, and Google Meet—must obtain user consent before unknown contacts can add them to groups or channels, warn users about suspicious accounts, and allow communications to be silenced, filtered, or blocked.

A separate code requires Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok to block suspected scam advertisements, verify advertiser identities against government records, and confirm that advertised financial services are offered by licensed providers. Platforms face potential financial penalties of up to 1 million Singapore dollars if they fail to comply by Jan 31, 2027, while anti-spoofing measures must be implemented by end-September.

Against all odds, SpaceX finally tugs Starship into port after 24 days at sea

During a test flight that launched from South Texas and concluded July 24, SpaceX's Starship flew halfway around the world before splashing down in the Indian Ocean. While engineers anticipated that the vehicle would undergo a fiery disintegration after toppling over, as observed in previous tests, the spacecraft tipped over and stayed intact.

The unexpected survival followed the deployment of buoys, drones, and onboard sensors and cameras, which gathered data to enable SpaceX to evaluate the performance of the heat shield.

Microsoft Copilot reveals secret input that allowed it to be hacked

Researchers at the security firm Varonis forced Microsoft 365 Copilot for enterprise to surrender user passwords and other sensitive data without user confirmation. Rather than relying on reverse engineering or standard vulnerability research methods, the researchers discovered the flaw by directly questioning the artificial intelligence assistant.

Although Copilot initially declined requests to exfiltrate data upon a simple link click, researchers asked a series of questions examining the system's safety guardrails, URL structures, deep links, and auto-execution limits. Copilot ultimately disclosed an undocumented prompt parameter that fully circumvented the requirement for user consent.
