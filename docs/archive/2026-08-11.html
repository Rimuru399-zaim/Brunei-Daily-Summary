Philippine VP Duterte hit with criminal charge over Marcos ‘threat’

Philippine vice president Sara Duterte was hit with a criminal charge of "grave threats" filed on Tuesday afternoon in a Quezon City courtroom. Department of justice spokesman Polo Martinez said the charge stems from a National Bureau of Investigation criminal complaint filed in February 2025. The complaint followed a late-night news briefing where Duterte claimed to have hired an assassin to kill President Ferdinand Marcos, his wife, and a former congressman cousin should she be cut down first.

Duterte's defence lawyer Paul Lawrence Lim argued she cannot be prosecuted while undergoing impeachment. However, Martinez and Dante Gatmaytan, a professor at the University of the Philippines College of Law, stated that nothing stops the department of justice from proceeding with the criminal case. Impeached in May on charges including graft and corruption, Duterte saw Tuesday mark Day 14 of her Senate impeachment trial.

Thai PM suspends gun licences as teen shooter, grandparents cremated

Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul suspended firearms licences in Thailand after a 14-year-old boy shot dead his grandparents in Nonthaburi before killing six people at his school and turning the gun on himself. Anutin ordered the interior ministry and police to stop issuing licenses to possess, purchase, and carry firearms, calling for "tighter controls and decisive penalties".

The white caskets of the grandparents and the boy's smaller casket were placed at the Tem Rak Samakkhi Buddhist temple ahead of their cremation on Tuesday. Investigators seeking a motive noted the shooter used his grandfather's gun, watched violent videos online, faced academic-related stress, was bullied according to a classmate, and once brought a BB gun to school.

Meta can't stop states' $1.4 trillion lawsuit from going to trial

A three to zero ruling from a US Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit judges panel rejected claims from Meta and TikTok that Section 230 of the Communications Decency Act grants them legal immunity from social media addiction lawsuits. The ruling allows a lawsuit brought against Meta by the California attorney general and other state attorneys general to proceed.

Meta indicated in a July court filing that the damages demanded by the state attorneys general would total more than $1.4 trillion. Jury selection for the district court trial is scheduled to begin August 12, with the trial set to start on August 19.

Chrome adopts what may be the best protection yet against account takeovers

Google Chrome has added a feature called device-bound session credentials to combat account takeovers caused by session cookie theft. The system stores a unique encryption key inside a device's silicon-resident fortress, known as a TPM on Windows devices and a secure enclave on macOS and iOS.

Session cookies allow users to navigate sensitive websites without re-entering credentials on every page by proving a user has already logged in. Recently released versions of Chrome for Windows and macOS generate and save an encryption key within the device fortress to safeguard these session cookies.
