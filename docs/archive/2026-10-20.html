INDONESIA FOREST FIRES

Police Probe Foreign Involvement in 98 Corporate Fire Cases

*Authorities examine foreign links in land fires as officials push to intensify firefighting operations.*

By the Dispatch Desk

Indonesian police have confirmed foreign involvement, including from neighbouring countries, in 98 corporate cases connected to the forest and land fires currently gripping the country.

Speaking after a limited Cabinet meeting with President Prabowo Subianto in Jakarta on Monday, National Police Chief General Suyudi Ario Seto said the Criminal Investigation Agency is working to identify those responsible and establish the nature of their involvement. Investigators are collecting evidence and expect to name suspects soon, including company owners and shareholders.

"Of the 98 corporate cases we are investigating, there is foreign involvement, but as the investigations are ongoing, we are still determining which involve foreign investment or foreign parties and which do not," Suyudi said.

Separately, Coordinating Minister for Political and Security Affairs Dudung Abdurachman called for firefighting efforts to be stepped up without waiting for rainfall, noting that the president instructed deployments of the military, police, Forestry Service, and members of the public to be intensified on a massive scale.

Dudung said Indonesia has responded to offers of foreign assistance from countries including Malaysia and Singapore, with the National Disaster Management Agency coordinating technical arrangements. However, foreign aid will have to wait until the government declares a national disaster, a step it has yet to take while maintaining it can manage the crisis using its own resources.

Three in Four Singaporeans Say Their Spare Cash Isn’t Working Hard Enough

A survey of 1,000 Singapore residents commissioned by Chocolate Finance and conducted by Milieu found that three in four individuals with spare cash believe it is not working as hard as it could. While 89 percent of respondents hold spare cash, 48 percent reported checking reward points or cashback over the preceding six months, compared with 37 percent who monitored interest rates on their spare cash.

The study also found that only 11 percent of respondents are very confident they know the interest rates paid by their bank accounts, while 72 percent either view their spare funds as money for an indefinite future date or do not consider timing at all. In response, Chocolate Finance announced plans to introduce a feature called Jars in November 2026 to help users allocate spare cash into categories with defined goals and timeframes.

Startup taps underwater volcano as energy source in "proof point" test

A Seattle-based startup, Endurance Energy, conducted the world's first demonstration of a subsea geothermal power plant almost a mile beneath the surface near the Axial Seamount, an active underwater volcano in the northeast Pacific Ocean. During the three-week test deployment in September, the 3-ton prototype generated less than 10 seconds of net-positive electricity, showing the technical feasibility of subsea geothermal power.

Endurance Energy aims to commercialize the technology for Pacific island nations and customers seeking alternatives to costly imported fossil fuels. Company founder and CEO Andrew Redd explained that the deep ocean offers unlimited cold seawater for cooling while the water's weight maintains pressure on the system and well, allowing the plants to remain compact.

Stratospheric airship flew from US to Japan to test sky cell tower concept

Sceye, a US aerospace company based near Albuquerque, New Mexico, completed a round-trip trans-Pacific flight from New Mexico to Japan and back between August and September 2026. The flight demonstrated how an uncrewed, helium-filled lighter-than-air platform can serve as a cell phone tower in the sky. Powered by onboard batteries and top-mounted solar panels, the platform uses an electric-powered propeller to resist strong winds and hover over a single location.

According to Sceye founder and CEO Mikkel Frandsen, the craft operates continuously in the stratosphere at approximately 60,000 feet, which places it twice as high as commercial air traffic, above the jet stream, and thirty times closer than satellites in low-Earth orbit. The system flies day and night, using solar energy each morning to recharge its batteries while remaining over the operational area at the same altitude.
