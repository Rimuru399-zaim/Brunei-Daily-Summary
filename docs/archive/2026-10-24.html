Bruneian named youngest judge at Malaysia sustainability awards

Bruneian youth leader Muhammad Fadli bin Haji Awang Zaini was appointed as the sole international judge and youngest member of the judging panel for the JCI Malaysia Sustainable Development Awards 2026, which concluded with a ceremony in Miri, Sarawak. Representing the Young Professionals Network Brunei, Fadli evaluated initiatives contributing towards the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals alongside Karambir Singh, Ir Jess Vun, and Dr Gwee Sai Ling. The ceremony marked the 10th anniversary of the awards and was attended by Sarawak Minister for Transport Dato Sri Lee Kim Shin and Miri Mayor Adam Yii Siew Sang.

Organised by Junior Chamber International Malaysia and hosted by JCI Lutong, the competition received 49 submissions, shortlisted 29 finalists, and presented 10 awards. Fadli, who took part in judging sessions on August 29 and 30, noted that sustainable development should be assessed by its meaningful impact on communities rather than simply the number of programmes delivered or people reached.

ASEAN

Singapore widens haze probe to local firms linked to Indonesian fires

Singapore has widened its inquiry into companies connected to fires in Indonesia that have produced toxic regional haze. The National Environmental Agency is seeking information from 10 Singapore-registered palm oil and forestry firms regarding land concession locations, fire prevention and firefighting measures, and ties to associated businesses in Singapore and Indonesia. The city-state has pledged legal action, having already issued notices to 10 foreign-based companies demanding an end to burning and the deployment of firefighting teams, and plans to form a panel of audit, legal, and investigative firms to assist with cross-border enquiries.

The blazes, exacerbated by peatland drainage, deforestation, and a strong El Nino cycle, have burned nearly two million hectares across forests, peatlands, and plantations, according to Nusantara Atlas. Although civil society groups have accused companies of responsibility for hotspots within supplier concessions, implicated businesses named by Singapore authorities, including First Resources and Asia Pulp and Paper, maintain they are not behind the fires. Air monitor IQAir placed both Singapore and Kuala Lumpur among the 10 most polluted major cities late Friday.

Thailand welcomes US-Myanmar engagement

Thai foreign minister and deputy prime minister Sihasak Phuangketkeow stated that Thailand welcomes engagement between the United States and Myanmar, following talks between Washington and Myanmar's military-backed government in Tokyo. Sihasak noted that Bangkok does not want Myanmar isolated, explaining that contact is intended to encourage reconciliation and peaceful conflict resolution rather than confer legitimacy on the regime. He added that the upcoming Asean summit in Manila will feature substantive discussions on how to proceed with the member, which has been barred since the 2021 coup.

Sihasak also indicated that Thailand and Cambodia could resolve their longstanding land and maritime boundary disputes within 12 to 18 months, despite limited progress to date and the need to address border security concerns first. The maritime dispute involves overlapping claims to approximately 26,000 sq km in the Gulf of Thailand, believed to contain oil and gas reserves valued around US$300 billion. Additionally, Sihasak noted that Thailand-EU free-trade negotiations have reached their final stage and could conclude by year-end if there is mutual political will.

Moderna and Merck say their mRNA cancer vaccine cut death, recurrence by 42%

In a late-stage trial, a personalized mRNA vaccine against melanoma reduced the risk of death or recurrence by 42 percent compared to standard treatment, pharmaceutical partners Merck and Moderna announced Thursday. Merck and Moderna are already engaging with regulatory authorities to get the vaccine approved.

Moderna CEO Stéphane Bancel stated that the trial results show what is possible when rethinking how medicines are made, adding that the outcome reinforces conviction that mRNA technology has the potential to fundamentally change cancer treatment.
