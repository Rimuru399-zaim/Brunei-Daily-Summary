US-CANADA TRADE

US and Canada Reach Trade Agreement, Trump Says

*Donald Trump announced that the United States and Canada reached a deal to prevent tariffs on American exports.*

By the Dispatch Desk

United States President Donald Trump said the US has reached "a very fair" trade agreement with Canada that will ensure no tariffs on exports from US businesses and farmers.

The announcement came after Trump placed a three-day pause on 50 percent tariffs that had been scheduled to take effect on Wednesday, following discussions with Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney. Trump stated on Truth Social that the pause was enacted based on the two countries reaching a deal, subject to the finalization of documents.

Carney said in a statement that "substantial progress has been made, although there is important work still to be done". Full details of the trade agreement have not been confirmed by officials from either nation.

The threatened tariffs would have applied to roughly $20bn in imports, including goods qualifying for preferential treatment under the US-Mexico-Canada trade agreement. The automotive industry served as a central sticking point during the negotiations.

In meetings involving US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, officials discussed lowering Section 232 vehicle tariffs from 25 percent to 15 percent, with additional reductions tied to the volume of US content. Nearly 72 percent of Canada's goods exports went to the US last year.

mRNA cancer vaccine succeeded in Phase 3 melanoma trial, Moderna and Merck say

Moderna and Merck announced on Wednesday that their individualized mRNA-based vaccine proved effective in a Phase 3 clinical trial involving patients with melanoma. The study evaluated 1,137 individuals with surgically removed stage IIB–IV melanoma to assess the treatment.

The double-blind, placebo-controlled trial randomized participants to receive either Merck's Keytruda alone or Keytruda combined with the tailored mRNA vaccine, designated as intismeran or mRNA-4157, over a treatment period of approximately one year.

FCC abolishes gigabit speed goal, suggesting it is unfair to slower technologies

The Federal Communications Commission has eliminated the gigabit broadband speed goal established during the Biden administration, determining that current United States broadband deployment levels are acceptable. The regulator stated that the gigabit objective was not "technologically neutral" and disadvantaged slower technologies.

The decision was finalized on August 14 in the FCC's broadband deployment report mandated by Section 706 of the Telecommunications Act. The abolition of the long-term target, which had called for 1Gbps download and 500Mbps upload speeds, was proposed last year by Chairman Brendan Carr.
