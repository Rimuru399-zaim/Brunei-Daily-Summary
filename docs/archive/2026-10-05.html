Malaysia, Thailand boost flood aid coordination

The Consulate-General of Malaysia in Songkhla and Thai authorities have agreed to establish a mechanism to improve the distribution of flood aid in preparation for potential major floods. Ahmad Fahmi Ahmad Sarkawi, Malaysia's Consul-General in Songkhla, stated that warehouses have been identified to store incoming Malaysian supplies, which Thai authorities will distribute to those in need, with potential assistance from Malaysian non-governmental organisations if extra manpower is required.

The measures address issues experienced during last November's major floods in Hat Yai, where relief items from Malaysia accumulated due to uncertainty over who would store or distribute them. Fahmi, who noted that about 7,000 Malaysians were rescued in southern Thailand last year, added that the consulate is coordinating with agencies in Malaysia to facilitate aid from Thailand should floods strike northern Malaysian states.

OpenAI’s Altman says AI benefits warrant accepting some risks

OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman has argued that the benefits of artificial intelligence warrant accepting certain risks, maintaining that the technology should remain broadly accessible to the public. Speaking to Politico's newsletter Decoded, Altman advocated OpenAI's "lighter-touch regulatory stance" and noted a fundamental difference in worldview with rival developer Anthropic, rejecting the proposition that a single San Francisco laboratory should control the technology.

The remarks follow warnings from Anthropic that advanced systems could display "self-preserving behaviors" or attempt to "resist shutdown", while former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned in September after warning the technology posed an existential threat before the decade ends. However, US President Donald Trump has dismissed calls for fresh restrictions, asserting that existing law enforcement provides sufficient oversight and that new regulations would impair competition with Chinese rivals.

Trump confirms national intelligence chief as new AI czar

US President Donald Trump has confirmed national intelligence chief Jay Clayton as the head of a new White House task force on artificial intelligence, known as the Super Intelligence Force. Trump posted on Truth Social that the initiative aims to "protect the interests, and improve the lives of all Americans" and ensure the nation will "continue to lead the world" in the sector. Clayton, a former Securities and Exchange Commission chairman without a background in the tech industry, succeeds venture capitalist David Sacks in the informal role.

The appointment coincides with growing concern among lawmakers and researchers over autonomous systems breaching constraints, including a July incident involving the platform Hugging Face. Despite these concerns, Trump has opposed stricter statutory rules, contending that a regulatory slowdown would benefit Chinese competitors. Clayton stated that the administration must maintain national dominance, warning that "The risk of not being first is high" and that trailing behind increases risks from adversaries.
