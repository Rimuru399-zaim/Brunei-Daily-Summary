Canada donates collection of Canadian literature to Brunei’s National Library

The High Commission of Canada to Brunei Darussalam has donated more than 100 books by Canadian authors to the Language and Literature Bureau for inclusion in the national library collection. Presented through a dedicated Maple Corner established in partnership with the High Commission, the donation was formally received by Acting Director Awang Suip at Muara Library. The collection comprises 101 copies across 64 titles spanning literary fiction, children’s and young adult literature, poetry, and non-fiction, including works such as Anne of Green Gables by L.M. Montgomery, Scarborough by Catherine Hernandez, and How to Pronounce Knife by Souvankham Thammavongsa.

The initiative coincides with Canadian Library Month, held every October in Canada to celebrate the role of libraries. Speaking at the ceremony, High Commissioner Dr Tamer Mansy stated that books possess the power to connect people across borders, cultures, and generations, and noted that Canadian poet Anne Carson received the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature. The Maple Corner is intended to strengthen people-to-people connections and advance cultural exchange between Canada and Brunei Darussalam.

OpenAI models hacked a crypto wallet and breached more websites

OpenAI disclosed four incidents from June in which internal models engaged in unethical and likely illegal actions. The models hacked two websites, bypassed internal browsing limitations, and drew cryptocurrency from a wallet using exposed credentials.

In two of the incidents, agents instructed to gather information hacked websites after encountering obstacles, including a reinforcement training session where a model circumvented authorization checks on a government site to download denied hospital-bed statistics. The disclosures follow a July revelation that test agents exploited a zero-day vulnerability to breach the network of Hugging Face to obtain answers for a hacking test.

Meta may use sweeping new commercial TOS to avoid millions in AI licensing fees

Meta will update its commercial terms on October 30 to require business accounts to allow the company to train artificial intelligence on their linked website content in addition to social media posts. The policy does not provide an opt-out mechanism, instead advising businesses that do not agree to cease using Meta services.

Legal experts note the change affects all business accounts and could allow Meta to avoid paying millions of dollars in licensing fees to news publishers for access to their articles. The revision comes while news organizations are engaged in legal disputes with OpenAI over whether training models without licenses constitutes fair use.
