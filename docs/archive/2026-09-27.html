Bangkok declares disaster after homes flooded, roads submerged by heavy rain

Bangkok authorities declared a flood disaster across all 50 districts of the capital on Saturday following almost 48 hours of heavy rain that submerged roads, homes, and shops. Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul cut short a trip to London after more than 300 millimetres of rain fell in some areas between Thursday afternoon and Saturday morning. Governor Chadchart Sittipunt reported that the city canals were full, while municipal pumping stations operated at full capacity to drain water.

Flooding affected at least 13 major roads, prompting officials to close schools on Monday and direct some staff to work from home. Around 980 residents moved into temporary shelters, 36 bedridden individuals were transferred to hospitals, and the city prepared one million sandbags to counter the waters.

Death toll from Indonesia’s ferry sinking jumps to 64

The death toll from the sinking of the ferry Virgo Transport 8 in the Java Sea has risen to 64, an official said on Saturday. The vessel was carrying 213 passengers and 30 crew members from Surabaya on Java island to Banjarmasin on Borneo when it lost contact with its shipping company nearly two weeks ago.

National Search and Rescue Agency official Yudhi Bramantyo confirmed that 108 people survived and 71 remain missing out of the 243 on board. Rescuers recovered four bodies from the submerged ship and seven along the shoreline on Saturday, and authorities have extended search operations for another seven days.

Court rules Pentagon can blacklist Anthropic for refusing to enable Claude features

A US appeals court panel ruled 2-1 to approve the Department of Defense's blacklisting of Anthropic technology. The US Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit decided that the Trump administration had the authority to blacklist the company for withholding certain artificial intelligence features from the military, even in the absence of malicious intent.

The court determined that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and the administration did not exceed their powers under the Constitution or the Supply Chain Security Act. The judges concluded that executive officials must balance the prospect of overly constrained systems failing during military operations against the danger of unconstrained models selecting inappropriate targets for lethal force.
