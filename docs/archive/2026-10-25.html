Brunei completes 10 liver transplants, expanding access to specialist care

Brunei has completed 10 living donor liver transplants since introducing its local programme in February 2025, conducted by the Ministry of Health alongside Jerudong Park Medical Centre. The initiative allows patients to undergo transplantation domestically, with local doctors collaborating with visiting specialists. Dr Taufique Ahmed, lead of the Liver Transplant Programme, stated that an estimated 60% to 70% of these patients would previously have been unable to receive care because they were too unwell to travel overseas or faced other clinical challenges, whereas the ministry previously sent only one or two patients abroad annually.

The procedure relies on the liver's capacity to regenerate. A section of a healthy donor's liver is surgically removed and transplanted into a recipient to replace a diseased liver, after which the liver tissue in both individuals regrows to normal size and assumes typical function within a couple of months.

Ensnared female orangutan and her baby rescued in Indonesia

Indonesian conservation workers on the island of Borneo rescued an ensnared 25-year-old female orangutan named Gadis and her 10-month-old son, Garis. Local conservation official Muriansyah reported that the mother sustained deep wounds to her arms from ropes that appeared to have been set near a Central Kalimantan village as a wild boar snare trap. Both animals were sent for medical care to the Orangutan Foundation International clinic near Pangkalan Bun.

While rope snares are commonly used outside protected reserves to guard crops against wild boars, officials have warned that careless placement threatens protected wildlife. The rescue comes as critically endangered orangutans face additional pressure from widespread fires across Indonesia, with the International Union for Conservation of Nature noting that blazes have burned in habitats supporting 52 percent of the orangutan population on Borneo.

AI’s ‘opportunity divide’ could deepen global inequality, UN official warns

Tomas Lamanauskas, deputy secretary-general of the International Telecommunication Union, warned at the UNCTAD World Investment Forum in Doha, Qatar, that artificial intelligence risks widening the divide between rich and poor nations. Lamanauskas pointed to significant infrastructure disparities, noting that Africa and Latin America together account for 3.3 percent of global data centre capacity, while 2.2 billion people worldwide remain without internet access. Bridging this digital infrastructure gap will require an estimated $2.8 trillion in investment by 2030.

Adoption rates also reflect this disparity, with generative AI used by approximately 29 percent of working-age people in developed nations compared to 16 percent in developing countries. A December 2025 report by the UN Development Programme similarly warned that artificial intelligence could trigger a new era of inequality, particularly for countries facing unreliable power, limited connectivity, and a lack of technological skills.
