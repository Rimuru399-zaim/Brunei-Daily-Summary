Controlling the brain with light earns a physiology Nobel

The Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine has been awarded to Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann, and Georg Nagel for developing optogenetics, a field that uses light to alter the behavior of nerve cells marked by individual gene activity. Early methods for understanding specialized cells in the brain and spinal cord involved genetically deleting neurons, but the brain could adapt to the loss of cells or experience altered development in connected neurons.

Beginning with research into single-celled algae that are attracted to light, researchers developed the ability to activate and shut down neurons inside an intact brain, providing a more informative method of study than previous deletion techniques or examinations of damaged brain regions.

AI glasses face their first major government crackdown

Norway's center-left government has proposed a temporary ban on AI glasses in certain public places, making it the first major country to pursue such a measure amid privacy concerns regarding wearable technology. The government plans to introduce legislation to parliament covering locations including beaches, parks, schools, and kindergartens, while an expert group will be established to draft permanent national regulations.

Torgeir Micaelsen, Norway’s digitalization minister, stated that he is concerned about individuals risking being photographed, filmed, or recorded without their knowledge through powerful new technology. Micaelsen noted that an interim restriction would allow time for a thorough assessment and an informed debate to guide permanent regulation.

MCP for agent-to-agent comms may be the riskiest protocol you've never heard of

The widespread adoption of AI agents across millions of organizations has opened avenues for attackers to prompt harmful actions, such as extracting sensitive business and personal data as well as database contents. Over the past five months, Google and four other entities have acknowledged vulnerabilities involving a specialized form of prompt injection, where an internal agent receives harmful instructions and passes them down the chain to other agents that explicitly trust it.

Independent researcher Syed Anas Mohiuddin conducted tests on agents from organizations such as Google, JP Morgan Chase, Weviate, Rapid7, the US federal government, and the French government's interministerial digital directorate. His proof-of-concept attacks took advantage of trust gaps in the Model Context Protocol, or MCP, a communication standard utilized by AI applications and agents within internal networks.
