Indonesia seizes 1.3 tonnes of ketamine worth millions in major bust

Indonesian authorities intercepted a foreign-flagged vessel near the Riau Islands and seized 1.3 tonnes of ketamine valued at 2.07 trillion rupiah. National Narcotics Agency chief Suyudi Ario Seto stated that a navy warship stopped the ship following months of surveillance triggered by intelligence received in February. Search teams discovered 65 hidden sacks containing the substance, resulting in the detention of eight crew members consisting of seven Myanmar nationals and one Taiwanese national.

The country enforces strict anti-drug legislation that includes the death penalty for traffickers, though a moratorium on executions has been maintained for several years. This interception follows earlier enforcement actions, including the seizure of 3.37 metric tonnes of cannabis buds from Thailand last month and a 2021 bust involving 2.5 tonnes of methamphetamine originating from Afghanistan.

Mount Toba eruption doesn't seem like it could nearly kill our species

Mount Toba, located on what is now Sumatra, experienced the largest volcanic eruption in the last 2.6 million years approximately 74,000 years ago. Thousands of cubic kilometers of magma emptied in roughly two weeks, which led some to believe it nearly caused human extinction through severe global cooling. However, geoscientist Jinheum Park and colleagues from Johannes Gutenberg University examined mud from a crater lake on the Kenya-Tanzania border and discovered the eruption caused only about half a degree of cooling, lasting less than two years.

Large volcanic eruptions inject sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, forming droplets that reflect sunlight. While bigger eruptions eject more sulfur dioxide, this cooling trend stops at extreme magnitudes because heavier sulfate aerosols settle quickly, reducing their ability to scatter solar radiation.

The first self-driving vehicle on Mars has proven to be a smashing success

NASAs Perseverance rover is set to break the record for the farthest distance driven on another world, exceeding 45.16 km across Mars since landing in February 2021. This distance surpasses the previous record set by the Opportunity rover, which stopped communicating in 2018. Perseverance achieved this mark faster largely because of its auto navigation system and Vision Compute Element, which enable autonomous processing while driving. About 90 percent of its total distance has been driven autonomously.

Project manager Steven Lee and deputy project scientist Vivian Sun noted that autonomous navigation optimizes scientific output by reducing wait times for Earth-based commands. In comparison, the older Curiosity rover has driven 38.6 km over more than a decade, with only about 10 percent of its distance driven autonomously due to slower processing hardware. Perseverance maintains a maximum wheel speed of approximately 150 meters per hour.

DeepMind’s hurricane breakthrough has surprised weather scientists

In October 2025, an artificial intelligence model called WeatherNext, created by Googles DeepMind and Google Research, predicted five days in advance with 80 percent confidence that a storm would hit Jamaica as a Category 5 hurricane. Hurricane Melissa subsequently caused severe flooding and landslides across Jamaica, but the early AI prediction provided extra lead time for local communities to prepare.

Research published in Nature demonstrated that WeatherNext forecasts cyclones with unprecedented accuracy, offering forecasters an average of one extra day of lead time compared to existing models. Consequently, its three-day forecasts achieve the same level of accuracy as two-day predictions from previous systems.
