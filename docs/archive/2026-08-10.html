107,000ha of forest, land affected by fires in Indonesia as haze fear grows

Fires across Indonesia have affected more than 107,000ha of forest and land as of Monday, prompting the government to increase efforts to prevent transboundary haze. Coordinating Minister for Political and Security Affairs Djamari Chaniago stated that no reports have been received showing smoke affecting other nations. Aerial and ground operations have been deployed, including 43 helicopters, fixed-wing aircraft, equipment, and weather modification measures.

Authorities are closely monitoring six provinces, including Riau, Jambi, South Sumatra, West Kalimantan, Central Kalimantan, and South Kalimantan. Strong El Nino conditions, which can accelerate forest and land fires, are expected to persist until around September or early October.

Days of heavy rain leave at least 13 dead in Philippines

Heavy rains exacerbated by tropical storms have killed at least 13 people in the northern Philippines following a nine-day downpour. The severe weather triggered floods, landslides, and accidents, forcing the closure of schools and government offices across Luzon. In the mountain resort of Baguio, a rain-drenched mountainside collapsed on several houses, killing four residents and leaving at least six others missing.

Other fatalities on Luzon included six people killed by landslides, one person who drowned, one struck by a falling tree, and one electrocuted. Public works secretary Vince Dizon stated that floodwaters were receding, while acknowledging the necessity to repair faulty flood mitigation projects that have cost taxpayers billions of dollars.

Technology

Following Epic loss, Google has started hosting rival app stores in the Play Store

Following an antitrust lawsuit won by Epic in 2023, Google has started hosting a third-party app store, Aptoide, within Google Play for users in the United States. Judge James Donato ordered remedies that required Google to distribute alternative app stores, lower developer fees, permit alternative payment systems, and mirror Google Play apps in other app stores.

Google and Epic had previously agreed to a settlement announced early this year to certify third-party app stores rather than host them in Google Play. That agreement was withdrawn when it appeared the court would not approve it, forcing Google to host the rival store in the United States to comply with the judicial order.

A researcher bought noreply.net. Companies started sending him secrets.

Security researcher Cory Solovewicz receives hundreds of misdirected emails daily containing private information and organizational secrets. Since December 2024, one domain owned by Solovewicz registered 401,796 messages, averaging 699.99 pings per day. The incoming messages have included city government injury reports, pizza order confirmations, school platform account setup emails, repair service orders, and credentials for test platforms.

Solovewicz acquired the domain noreply.us in 2020 and noreply.net in 2024. Although he initially intended to set up a catch-all email address on noreply.us to filter messages and enhance privacy, external systems continuously sent mail to addresses on the domain, creating an accidental honeypot.

Peer review is overwhelmed—can it survive in the AI era?

Jason Semprini, a health economist at Des Moines University, authored a research study examining policies mandating the human papillomavirus vaccine for elementary school students. Although human papillomavirus causes most cervical cancer cases, Semprini found that mandates do not do much to reduce overall cervical cancer rates in a population, as mandating a vaccine can motivate some people to find ways to avoid it.

After Semprini submitted the manuscript to a journal for peer review, a reviewer misunderstood the central message of the study. The reviewer mistakenly thought Semprini was questioning whether the vaccine itself prevents cervical cancer, rather than studying the effectiveness of a policy intended to increase vaccination rates.
