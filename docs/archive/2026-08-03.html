Loke resigns as Negri Sembilan DAP chief [WATCH]

DAP secretary-general Anthony Loke announced his resignation as the party's Negri Sembilan chief after DAP lost two seats in the Aug 1 state election, including Chennah and Repah. Loke, who held the state leadership position for 22 years, stated that he made the personal decision to step down to take responsibility for the electoral performance. He will remain as DAP secretary-general following a decision by the party's Central Executive Committee.

Loke noted that his resignation will take effect after he calls the Negri Sembilan DAP committee meeting this week to select a new state chairman. Reflecting on his tenure, he highlighted that the state party expanded from winning two seats when he took over in 2004 to holding two members of Parliament and retaining nine seats.

Aung San Suu Kyi dalam kemunculan pertama temui pegawai Palang Merah

Detained former Myanmar leader Aung San Suu Kyi met with Arnaud de Baecque, the permanent representative of the International Committee of the Red Cross to Myanmar, in Naypyidaw. Suu Kyi has been detained since a military coup in February 2021 and currently faces 18 years in prison following reduced sentences on multiple charges. An AI-supervised remote exam went so badly that 58,000 students must retake it

Nearly 160,000 applicants took a remote entrance exam for UNAM, Mexico's largest university, using webcam proctoring and browser security. Following a sharp increase in top scores compared to previous years, an expert commission recommended that around 58,000 applicants participate in an in-person control exam to secure university placement.

The sudden rise in high scores raised concerns of widespread cheating. The university rector apologized to honest test takers who must sit for the new exam, stating the action is required to ensure equity and certainty.

How headlights got brighter, whiter, and more blinding after dark

Modern automotive lighting has grown significantly brighter, causing intense glare for drivers operating vehicles at night. While observers often assume motorists are leaving their high beams on, the causes behind the increase in bright white vehicle lighting are more complex.

Before the advent of modern automobiles, horse-drawn carriages relied on oil lanterns along dark roadways outside towns and cities. Because these early lanterns provided limited illumination, after-dark collisions and near-misses occurred frequently.
