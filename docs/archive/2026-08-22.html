Indonesia deploys hundreds of soldiers to tackle Borneo fires

Indonesia is deploying an additional 1,500 soldiers to Borneo to help combat wildfires that burned over 200,000 hectares of land between January and July. President Prabowo Subianto ordered the troop deployment during a visit to the island on Saturday, and six helicopters were also scheduled to arrive to assist waterbombing operations.

The fires, driven by dry conditions associated with an El Nino weather pattern, have spread thick smog across parts of Borneo and prompted local authorities to advise residents to wear masks. Smoke has also crossed into Malaysian Borneo, where six areas in Sarawak recorded "unhealthy" air quality.

Myanmar airstrike on monastery kills 14

A Myanmar military airstrike on a monastery in Myaung township of the central Sagaing region killed 14 people on Friday, according to local rebel fighters who witnessed the aftermath. The morning attack struck a monastery hall where civilians aged between 50 and 70 were attending a meditation retreat marking Buddhist Lent.

The victims comprised 11 men and three women. An opposition fighter said aircraft conducted a second bombing while rescue efforts were underway to help people at the site.

Technology

Chinese regulators tell Tesla to fix nearly 3 million cars

Chinese regulators are requiring Tesla to address safety concerns across 2.98 million Model 3 and Model Y electric vehicles in China. The automaker must add interior warning labels clarifying how to open rear doors during emergencies involving power loss, an intervention that requires physical access to the vehicles. Tesla will also issue a software update designed to automatically lower all windows if a collision occurs.

The measures follow regulatory scrutiny over electronically controlled doors after crashes resulted in occupants becoming trapped inside burning vehicles. While new regulations establishing specific handle dimensions for newly approved cars take effect next year, existing models remain grandfathered until at least the end of 2028.

Waymo doubles spending on lobbying in robotaxi battle with Uber

Waymo has increased its federal lobbying expenditure to more than $1 million between April and June, more than doubling its spending from the previous year. The Alphabet-owned firm is seeking regulatory approval for fully autonomous taxi operations, placing its outlay near that of Uber and ahead of competitors such as Tesla and Amazon's Zoox.

The increased spending highlights diverging strategies between the two companies. Waymo is pursuing a faster timeline for fully driverless commercial fleets, whereas Uber favors a staggered approach where robotaxis function alongside human drivers.
