INDONESIA EARTHQUAKE

Aid Reaches Flores Island as Quake Death Toll Rises to 51

Thousands of evacuees remain displaced on hilltops and in temporary shelters following a powerful 7.7-magnitude tremor and hundreds of aftershocks.

By the Dispatch Desk

Help began arriving on Sunday for thousands of evacuees on Indonesia's Flores island after a 7.7-magnitude earthquake killed 51 people, injured at least 101, and forced approximately 5,000 residents to flee their homes early Saturday.

The shallow earthquake struck off the island's north coast and was followed by at least 341 aftershocks. The disaster cut electricity and telecommunications across several areas and left at least one emergency access road impassable, while waves of up to 1.6 metres were recorded before an initial tsunami warning was lifted.

Berton Suar Pelita Panjaitan, a spokesman for the BNPB national disaster agency, said at least 914 houses were severely damaged, along with 93 educational facilities, 36 health facilities, and 38 government offices. Displaced residents taking shelter on hilltops and in makeshift camps reported urgent shortages of food, drinking water, medicines, and tents.

To support relief efforts, BNPB head Suharyanto confirmed the deployment of at least five helicopters, several planes, a naval vessel, and search and rescue ships. The Indonesian government also dispatched 50,000 aid packages weighing 275 tonnes, while European Union chief Ursula von der Leyen offered assistance using Copernicus Earth observation satellites.

In the town of Ruteng, heavy machinery cleared debris from streets while local hospital staff treated patients in outdoor tents after their facility sustained heavy damage. Local authorities have urged residents to stay alert and avoid returning to compromised buildings while aftershocks persist.

DAP congress votes to stay in govt

A majority of delegates at a DAP special congress held at the Putrajaya International Convention Centre voted for the party to remain in the government. Out of 2,099 votes counted, 1,857 delegates or 88 percent voted to stay, while 242 voted to leave. The attendance of 2,223 delegates represented about 52.12 percent of eligible voters, which secretary-general Loke Siew Fook described as high.

Loke stated that Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim had discussed cooperation with Umno and Barisan Nasional with the leadership of Pakatan Harapan in order to maintain government stability. Addressing delegates who wanted the party and the administration to be more decisive and assertive, Loke noted that their views would be conveyed to the government through the Cabinet.

Malaysia braces for stronger El Nino, severe haze

Malaysia is preparing for a stronger El Niño and severe haze as the southwest monsoon brings hotter and drier conditions. Natural Resources and Environmental Sustainability Minister Datuk Seri Arthur Joseph Kurup reported that unhealthy Air Pollutant Index readings were recorded in Johan Setia as well as Kuching, Samarahan, and the Serian police headquarters in Sarawak.

Preparations began on June 15 during a meeting of the National Haze and Dry Weather Committee, with measures outlined under the National Haze Action Plan and National Open Burning Action Plan. Regionally, Alert Level 2 was activated on Aug 3 under the Asean Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution, requiring member countries to provide daily reports on fires, haze, and countermeasures.
