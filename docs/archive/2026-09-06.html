VOLCANIC ERUPTION

Indonesia Shuts Eight Airports Following Anak Krakatoa Eruptions

*Roughly 170,000 passengers were left stranded after volcanic ash prompted the suspension of operations across eight airports.*

By the Dispatch Desk

About 170,000 passengers were stranded on Sunday after Indonesia closed its main international airport and seven others following eruptions at Mount Anak Krakatoa, which sent a plume of volcanic ash and lava more than 150 kilometres away. Across the eight affected airports, 1,558 flights were delayed, including more than 900 flights operating to and from Soekarno-Hatta International Airport.

The suspension of operations at Soekarno-Hatta was extended until 8.59am Monday "due to increased eruptive activity at Mount Anak Krakatoa", according to airport authorities. The closure also affected seven other airports until 8.59am, including Husein Sastranegara airport in Bandung and Radin Inten II airport near Bandar Lampung on Sumatra. Transportation Minister Dudy Purwagandhi said airports in cities including Semarang and Surabaya were prepared as alternative landing sites.

Mount Anak Krakatoa erupted on Saturday, producing a fountain of lava and booming sounds heard across several hundred kilometres, followed by two new eruptions recorded on Sunday. Ash from the volcano reached Bandar Lampung at the southern tip of Sumatra, blanketing parked cars and roadsides, while authorities announced that schools across Jakarta will close on Monday as classes shift online.

Local port authorities prohibited vessels from entering an exclusion zone within a three-kilometre radius of the volcano, instructing ships navigating the strait between Java and Sumatra to increase vigilance regarding navigation safety.

Geology agency head Lana Saria said "seismic activity indicates the movement or release of volcanic fluids within the volcanic system." She added that "the timing, magnitude, and nature of the next eruption cannot be precisely predicted," while noting that the recent eruptions do not currently have the potential to trigger a tsunami.

'Truly scared': Blazes outpace firefighters in Indonesian Borneo

Firefighters in Indonesian Borneo have struggled to contain widespread blazes fueled by an El Nino climate phenomenon, regional forest degradation, and slash-and-burn clearing practices. In Sintang regency, fire chief Sampe Ritonga reported that local responders were exhausted while operating with limited resources, including two 6,000-litre fire engines and a shortage of hoses, forcing crews to draw water from ditches and distant small rivers.

The fires have shrouded communities in thick haze, with PM2.5 air pollution in Pontianak exceeding 500 micrograms per cubic metre and leading to the indefinite closure of hundreds of schools across West Kalimantan. While the provincial government reported deploying nearly 14,500 personnel alongside aircraft for water-bombing and cloud-seeding, environmental campaigners maintain that the drainage of peatlands for monoculture plantations remains the root cause of the blazes.

Tesla’s Cybercab has been deployed, and it’s already under investigation

Tesla is preparing to transport members of the public in two states using the Cybercab, a driverless two-seater designed without a steering wheel or brake pedals. Tesla plans to introduce the vehicles to its Robotaxi ride-hail network, which currently operates in select cities in Texas and Florida. Following an event in downtown Austin where hundreds of fans were invited to ride in the vehicles, the US federal government opened an investigation into whether the model satisfies federal safety standards.

The probe coincides with efforts by the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration to update vehicle standards and facilitate the deployment of autonomous vehicles on public roads.
