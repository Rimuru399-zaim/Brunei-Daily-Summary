Philippines exploring deeper cooperation with Taiwan to protect cables

The Philippines is exploring deeper cooperation with Taiwan to protect undersea cables and address gray-zone activity, according to Felipe F. Carino III, Manila's deputy chief of mission in Washington. Speaking to the Center for a New American Security, Carino described this engagement as a "pragmatic and practical approach" to protect mutual interests in the Bashi Channel, despite Manila's one-China policy. He noted that undersea cables carry nearly all intercontinental data flows and linked cable security to South China Sea tensions, adding that "60-67% of cyber incidents happen in our region".

At the same seminar, Taiwan's deputy representative in Washington, Andrea Yang, noted the jailing in Taiwan of a Chinese ship captain over cable damage and stated that Taiwan would dedicate more resources to unmanned systems for maritime surveillance. Yang said interference with undersea cables should be addressed by the UNs to establish mechanisms against "bad behaviour". Earlier in the month, the Philippine coast guard deployed an aircraft and its largest vessel to challenge a Chinese research ship near undersea cable corridors off northern Batanes province.

'This is not Malaysia vs Indonesia', Syed Saddiq says amid haze row

Muar member of parliament Syed Saddiq Syed Abdul Rahman rejected framing the regional haze row as a dispute between nations, stating, "This is not Malaysia versus Indonesia". Responding to criticism from former Indonesian trade minister Muhammad Lutfi over his call for an Asean boycott of Indonesia, Syed Saddiq urged evidence-based action under Indonesian law against any Malaysian, Indonesian, or Singaporean company proven to have burned forests for profit. While debating the 2027 Budget, he had argued that funding should be cut to companies linked to open burning, insisting, "You must treat them as parties to be boycotted in this region".

Lutfi criticized the proposed boycott, stating that the haze crisis should not be used for cheap political publicity, and highlighted the deaths of Indonesian firefighters and the hundreds of thousands of people affected by smoke. Syed Saddiq maintained that large-scale plantation ownership alone was not proof of guilt and claimed that an offer by Malaysia to provide aircraft, personnel, and three helicopters to fight the fires in Indonesia had received no response.

OpenAI's latest AI models are coming to Google Cloud and Antigravity

Under a new arrangement between Google and OpenAI, the artificial intelligence company's frontier models will be incorporated into Google Cloud services and the Antigravity coding tool. The rollout will include the GPT 6 and 6.1 suite—Astra, Sol, and Luna—even as Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner.

The additions will be integrated into Google's Model Garden alongside Gemini, Claude, and open models including Mistral, Llama, and Gemma. Google stated that this inclusion makes it the only large cloud provider offering access to all three leading closed large language models, with a system that dynamically shifts workloads between models based on efficiency, latency, and performance.

Tesla profits drop 43% in Q3

Tesla generated $28 billion in revenue during the third quarter of 2026, roughly matching its total from the same period in 2025. Automotive revenue fell 3 percent year over year to $20.6 billion, and revenue from solar panels and storage batteries dropped 16 percent to $2.9 billion, though services and other revenues grew by a third to $4.6 billion.

Rising costs weighed heavily on profitability, as operating expenses climbed 39 percent to $4.8 billion. Consequently, operating margin fell from 5.8 percent a year earlier to 2.6 percent, while net profit dropped 43 percent to $787 million. Capital expenditures rose 110 percent to $4.7 billion, and free cash flow fell 62 percent to $1.5 billion.
