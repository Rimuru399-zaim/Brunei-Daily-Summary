BIBD launches landmark Sukuk to develop Brunei’s capital market

Bank Islam Brunei Darussalam has launched the BIBD Sukuk Wakalah Programme, marking the first corporate Sukuk Wakalah issuance in Brunei Darussalam. Institutional investors can take part directly in the primary issuance, while eligible retail investors can access the market through a fractional offering with a minimum investment of BND5,000.

The programme was launched jointly by Brunei Darussalam Central Bank Chairman Yang Berhormat Dato Seri Setia Dr Haji Abdul Manaf bin Haji Metussin and Bank Islam Brunei Darussalam Berhad Chairman Yang Berhormat Dato Seri Setia Awang Haji Ahmaddin bin Haji Abdul Rahman. The initiative aims to provide Shariah-compliant financing solutions to help organisations raise capital and diversify funding sources.

Baiduri Bank appoints new Group CEO

Baiduri Bank has appointed Dr Cédric Derras as its new Group Chief Executive Officer, succeeding Ti Eng Hui, who is set to retire on 25 October 2026 after three decades with the bank. Minister calls for greater understanding and support for mental health

Brunei Darussalam recorded nine reported suicide cases in 2025 compared with five in 2024, according to a World Mental Health Day 2026 message from Minister of Health Yang Berhormat Dato Seri Setia Dr Haji Mohammad Isham bin Haji Jaafar. In 2025, approximately 17,014 patients received treatment through psychiatric and psychological services nationwide, while the Talian Harapan 145 helpline received 2,515 calls, largely from people aged 18 to 35.

The country has previously strengthened its mental health framework through the 2014 Mental Health Act and the 2022 National Mental Health Action Plan. The Ministry of Health and the Ministry of Home Affairs are also collaborating to create the Brunei Darussalam Work-Life Balance Policy for All Workplaces, alongside providing basic mental health training for local administrators and religious personnel.

AutoPasar launches digital platform for new cars in Brunei

Wandering Ventures Sdn Bhd has launched AutoPasar, a free digital platform allowing car buyers in Brunei Darussalam to browse and compare 47 vehicle brands and 431 model variants. The website displays vehicle prices and estimated monthly instalments to assist buyers in a country where approximately 15,000 private cars are newly registered each year.

Over its initial three days, the platform drew 2,866 visitors and 31,577 page views, with 95 per cent of users located in Brunei and more than four in five browsing via mobile phones. Co-founder Toseef Ahmad stated that the resource gathers scattered vehicle pricing and cost information into a single location.

Haze: Malaysian, Singaporean firms under investigation

A Malaysian plantation group and several Singapore-linked business interests have been identified in connection with Indonesian concessions where fire hotspots were detected, according to a joint report by Greenpeace Indonesia, Greenpeace Malaysia, and Pantau Gambut. Although the findings did not accuse the firms of committing offenses or starting fires in Sumatra and West Kalimantan, Indonesian authorities have tied 57 businesses to open burning across 33,514 hectares, pursuing criminal investigations against 46 suspects and issuing administrative sanctions against 26 companies.

Amid calls for stronger regional cooperation, Malaysian Prime Minister Datuk Seri Anwar Ibrahim announced that Indonesian President Prabowo Subianto agreed to implement measures to tackle the worsening haze.

Ukraine’s drones knock out AI data center belonging to "Russia’s Google"

Ukrainian drone strikes disabled two of five data centers belonging to Russian tech company Yandex during attacks in the Sasovo and Kaluga regions on October 8 and October 9. NASA issues long-awaited call to industry for private space stations

On Friday, NASA issued a request to the US commercial space industry to develop private space stations to succeed the International Space Station as a habitat for astronauts in low-Earth orbit as early as 2030. In a cover letter accompanying the 360-page request for proposals, NASA stated that the plan is intended to ensure a sustained human presence in low-Earth orbit for crew members to perform science and exploration.

The document outlines capabilities sought by several private companies that have spent years designing and developing orbital stations. Industry responses are due back to NASA on December 8, with the agency planning to select two or more contractors in this phase and reach a final decision in April.

AI coding agents generate more code, but not more software

A study by Harvard University researchers Fiona Chen and James Stratton found little evidence that software development firms increase software output or reduce employment by using AI coding tools. Although assistants generate large quantities of functional code, the necessity of verifying accuracy creates a human code review bottleneck that absorbs initial efficiency gains through downstream production constraints.

The researchers observed that the code review process significantly increases in length, pull requests require more revisions, and reviewers leave more comments. The conclusions were based on Jellyfish analytics covering 300 million work events, including commits and pull requests, and issue management records across more than 700,000 employees at over 700 firms from 2021 through March of 2026.
