Indonesia ‘stepping up efforts’ to control Kalimantan fires

The Indonesian government is intensifying efforts to control raging forest and land fires in Kalimantan that have spread transboundary smoke haze to neighbouring countries, particularly Sarawak. Indonesia's national disaster management agency is resuming cloud-seeding to bring rain, strengthening ground operations with high-pressure pumps and protective gear, and maintaining 30 helicopters on standby for aerial patrols and water-bombing. Agency chief Lt Gen Suharyanto noted that fires in Kalimantan present distinct difficulties because many occur on peatlands, where embers can linger beneath the surface after flames appear extinguished.

Following instructions from President Prabowo Subianto, Indonesia announced the deployment of about 1,500 additional military personnel and six large water-bombing helicopters to West Kalimantan, where authorities identified 3,100 medium-confidence and 406 low-confidence hotspots. National secretariat minister Prasetyo Hadi stated that four corporations in West Kalimantan are under investigation over suspected violations, while Indonesian officials maintain formal and informal communications with neighbouring countries concerning the haze.

Due to need for 'absolute success,' China delays critical Moon launch to 2027

China has delayed its Chang’e 7 lunar exploration mission after determining that it does not meet the conditions for launch during its planned window this year. According to an announcement from the country's "Manned Space" program, the decision was made "In keeping with the principle of proceeding prudently and reliably, and ensuring absolute success". The statement provided no explanation for the cause of the delay from the Wenchang Space Launch Site.

The mission, which consists of an orbiter, lander, rover, and a mini-hopper probe, was scheduled to launch on a Long March 5 rocket and is designed to land at the South Pole of the Moon.

Humanoids break records at Beijing Robot Games

At the World Robot Games held at Beijing's Olympic speed skating arena, humanoid robots competed in events spanning athletics, boxing, football, and martial arts. During the event, a headless humanoid model named Tiangong Ultra ran the 100-metre dash in 9.39 seconds, surpassing Usain Bolt's record, and later completed the 400-metre sprint in 38.15 seconds.

The competitions also featured various mechanical failures, including robotic runners that failed to start, fell after the starting gun, or collapsed over boxing ropes. Spectator Hou Yali noted that while the machines demonstrated running speed, the contest did not test intelligence or the ability to perform household tasks.

Putting mice into hibernation causes a major loss of synapses

In a study published in Science, researchers led by neuroscientist Kazumasa Tanaka investigated memory retention by inducing a hibernation-like state in mice. Although this process erased the state of more than half of their synapses, the mice apparently retained their memories.

The technique relies on activating a population of Q neurons in the hypothalamus to trigger a neural circuit that is conserved across mammals. This method to artificially activate the hibernation circuit was developed in June 2020 by a team led by neuroscientist Takeshi Sakurai.
