Indonesia backs stronger Asean cooperation on transboundary haze

Indonesia has reaffirmed its commitment to the Asean Agreement on Transboundary Haze Pollution, with Indonesian Ambassador to Malaysia Datuk Raden Mohammad Iman Hascarya Kusumo calling for enhanced regional cooperation. Kusumo highlighted the need to strengthen implementation across early warning, real-time information sharing, firefighting coordination, weather modification, and capacity-building, while urging the full operationalisation of the Asean Coordinating Centre for Transboundary Haze Pollution Control.

The legally binding agreement was adopted by Asean member states in 2002 following recurring haze episodes, including the severe 1997-1998 crisis, to address pollution caused by land and forest fires. Indonesia became the final country among the ten member states to ratify the regional pact in 2015.

US trade curbs put Malaysia's solar, semiconductor ambitions to the test

New United States trade measures announced by President Donald Trump will impose a 15 per cent tariff and minimum import prices on polysilicon and its derivatives beginning Dec 4. Aimed at strengthening American domestic manufacturing and reducing reliance on China, the proposed price floor of US$21 per kilogramme sits significantly above the prevailing global spot price of roughly US$4.78 per kilogramme, creating both risks for Malaysia's solar industry and potential openings for its semiconductor sector.

Data from the Malaysian Investment Development Authority shows Malaysia maintains an ecosystem of about 250 solar-related companies across upstream and downstream activities. The Malaysia Photovoltaic and Sustainable Energy Industry Association noted that the sector comprises an export-oriented manufacturing base alongside a domestic deployment and services market driven by corporate renewable-energy demand and large-scale solar projects.

ChatGPT and Reddit now face EU's toughest online safety rules

The European Commission announced on Monday that OpenAI's ChatGPT, the social media forum Reddit, and the gaming platform Roblox will be classified as very large online platforms under the EU Digital Services Act.

Under the online safety framework, the three platforms must adhere to heightened obligations, including removing illegal content and protecting the privacy and security of minors. Failure to comply could result in fines of up to 6 percent of global revenue.

Sony, Warner Music sue Anthropic, saying it pirated songs to train its AI

The music publishing divisions of Sony Music and Warner Music have filed a lawsuit against Anthropic in United States federal court in California, alleging the company used pirated sheet music and lyrics from artists including The Beatles, Taylor Swift, and Michael Jackson to train its Claude artificial intelligence models. The complaint asserts that Anthropic acquired the copyrighted material via torrent downloads to generate competing lyric outputs.

The record labels are seeking injunctions to prevent Anthropic from using their works as well as statutory damages of up to $150,000 per infringed copyright. An Anthropic spokesperson stated on Monday that the company will defend itself "robustly" and maintained that artificial intelligence training constitutes fair use of copyrighted material.
