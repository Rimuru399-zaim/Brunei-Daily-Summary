Sarawak wants all pending MA63 issues resolved by December

The Sarawak government has urged Putrajaya to resolve all pending issues regarding the Malaysia Agreement 1963 by December this year. Speaking at the MA63 Implementation Action Council meeting, Sarawak deputy premier Douglas Uggah Embas noted that negotiations over full implementation have been ongoing for nearly a decade.

Delegates were informed that 13 matters have been fully resolved, including the transfer of Sabah Electricity Sdn Bhd to the Sabah government. Sabah and Sarawak affairs minister Mustapha Sakmud directed all federal ministries and agencies to prioritize settling the remaining issues to accelerate full implementation.

Indonesian shares outperform Southeast Asia peers, dollar recovery dents rupiah

Indonesian shares led regional gains on Tuesday as the Jakarta Composite Index rose 0.5 per cent to hit a near two-week high. Texas halts data center connections to power grid amid overwhelming demand

Less than a year after declaring Texas the "epicenter of AI development," Governor Greg Abbott announced a moratorium on new power grid connections for data centers. In an August 3 announcement, the Republican governor directed regulators at the Public Utility Commission of Texas and grid operators at the Electric Reliability Council of Texas to perform a "comprehensive verification and audit of all data centers advancing through ERCOT’s interconnection process."

ERCOT operates an independent power grid serving most of Texas separately from the rest of the United States. Texas has courted data center development with state incentives, cheap land, and abundant energy resources, putting the state on track to surpass Virginia as the largest US data center market.

US company’s AI lets Ukraine’s cheap kamikaze drones track targets on their own

Ukrainian operators have used $400 Shrike drones carrying explosive payloads to destroy Russian armored vehicles on the ground and military helicopters in midair. Thousands of these drones are now being upgraded with an artificial intelligence system that enables them to autonomously track and home in on moving targets.

In mid-July, the Ukrainian military began receiving SkyFall Shrike drones equipped with autonomy hardware and software from US firm Auterion. Auterion CEO Lorenz Meier stated that operators can manually fly the drones, designate a target up to half a mile away, and "flip the switch to turn it into fire-and-forget terminal guidance mode," with plans to deliver 50,000 equipped drones in the coming months.
