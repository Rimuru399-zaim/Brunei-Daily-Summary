BRUNEI ECONOMIC ASSESSMENT

AMRO Urges Brunei to Preserve Fiscal Buffers and Accelerate Diversification

The ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office reported that while Brunei maintains stable macroeconomic fundamentals, its heavy reliance on oil and gas leaves the economy exposed to global price volatility.

By the Dispatch Desk

Brunei Darussalam must continue safeguarding its fiscal buffers while stepping up economic diversification efforts to strengthen resilience against external shocks, according to an assessment by the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office. In a press release titled "Brunei Darussalam: Preserving Fiscal Buffers and Advancing Diversification for Economic Resilience", the organization highlighted that while macroeconomic fundamentals remain stable, the country's heavy reliance on the oil and gas sector continues to expose the economy to global commodity price volatility.

The assessment follows the organization's Annual Consultation Visit to Brunei held from June 10 to 18, 2026, where the mission evaluated the country's economic outlook and policy priorities. Brunei's economic growth is expected to moderate in the near term to a more sustainable pace, though the nation continues to benefit from low inflation and a strong external position.

A key recommendation from the report is the need to preserve fiscal buffers, which were built largely from hydrocarbon revenues and serve as a critical safeguard during downturns. The organization cautioned that prudent fiscal management, efficient public spending, and strengthened fiscal frameworks remain essential as global conditions become increasingly uncertain.

Advancing economic diversification was emphasized as central to enhancing resilience and reducing dependence on the energy sector. Key drivers of future growth include expanding non-oil and gas industries, attracting investment, supporting private sector development, improving productivity, and investing in human capital.

As the Sultanate progresses towards its "Wawasan 2035" aspirations, the report underscored the need for a balanced policy approach combining fiscal prudence with structural reforms to navigate global uncertainties while securing sustainable and inclusive growth.

BDCB warns public against AI-generated investment scams

Brunei Darussalam Central Bank has urged the public to remain vigilant against videos generated or manipulated using artificial intelligence to promote fraudulent investment schemes. The warning follows a video misusing the central bank's name and impersonating a senior government official. The central bank stated that it does not offer financial products or services to the public, nor does it promote or endorse investment platforms.

The central bank advised the public to deal only with licensed or regulated financial institutions and to verify information through official channels. It highlighted that the unauthorized use of its name or logo is an offence under the "Brunei Darussalam Central Bank Order, 2010", and urged reporting suspicious online content to its Financial Consumer Issues unit, relevant social media platforms, or the Brunei Computer Emergency Response Team.

Singapore govt takes possession of Lee Kuan Yew’s family home

The Singapore government has completed the acquisition process and taken possession of 38 Oxley Road, the family home of founding prime minister Lee Kuan Yew. The site was gazetted as a national monument on Dec 12 last year and gazetted for government acquisition on Jan 29. Lee's younger son, Hsien Yang, who was the last registered owner, criticized the compulsory acquisition, while he and his late sister Wei Ling had previously accused their elder brother Hsien Loong of wanting the bungalow preserved for political gain.

The Singapore Land Authority and the National Heritage Board announced they will undertake a detailed study on developing the site into a public space while respecting Lee Kuan Yew's wishes for privacy. Culture, community and youth minister David Neo previously stated that the government intends to turn the location into a public space such as a heritage park. A land authority spokesman noted that acquisition payment details could not be provided because such information is only available to the landowner.
