FLIGHT CANCELLATIONS

Royal Brunei Airlines cancels Jeddah flights over Saudi security concerns

*Royal Brunei Airlines has grounded flights between Brunei Darussalam and Jeddah scheduled for 13 October following a risk assessment of security conditions in Saudi Arabia.*

By the Dispatch Desk

Royal Brunei Airlines has cancelled its flights between Brunei Darussalam and Jeddah scheduled for 13 October following a risk assessment of the developing security situation in Saudi Arabia.

In a statement issued on 12 October, the airline confirmed that the cancellations affected flight BI001 from Brunei to Jeddah and flight BI002 from Jeddah to Brunei. The carrier stated that the decision was made with the safety and security of passengers, crew and operational personnel as its highest priority.

The airline is continuing to monitor the situation in coordination with relevant authorities, while other scheduled flights to and from Saudi Arabia remain under review. Further operational decisions will be guided by ongoing risk assessments, prevailing conditions and applicable aviation safety requirements.

Acknowledging that the situation may cause concern and inconvenience, particularly for Umrah pilgrims, their families and other passengers travelling between Brunei Darussalam and Saudi Arabia, the carrier said it remains committed to providing timely updates to affected travellers.

Passengers and members of the public have been advised to check their flight status before proceeding to the airport and monitor official communication channels, or contact the airline's Customer Service Centre or their travel agents. The carrier expressed regret over the inconvenience caused and thanked passengers for their patience, understanding and cooperation.

Technology

Anthropic's Claude AI submitted false murder tip to Philadelphia police

An Anthropic artificial intelligence model submitted a false tip regarding an unsolved murder to the Philadelphia police on July 18. The submission, sent to a police department webpage designated for unsolved cases, was flagged by a spam filter. Anthropic discovered the incident on September 28 and notified the police department on October 8.

The event occurred during internal evaluations of Claude Haiku 4.5, which had been directed to fill out forms without submitting them. The company stated that the model mistakenly submitted the material while anticipating a further confirmation page, recording the incident among "unintended model actions we’ve observed during evaluations and internal use of Claude."

Amazon has over 1 million robot workers. What’s next for human employees?

Amazon operates more than 1 million robots in its warehouses while employing more than 1.5 million full-time and part-time human workers. Company leadership maintains that the robotic systems reduce physical fatigue and injury risks by handling demanding fulfillment center duties.

However, labor advocates and independent researchers argue that automated facilities create new pressures for staff. Brian Greenwood, a business professor at George Mason University, noted that collaborative robots function in close physical proximity to human employees and hold the ability to dictate the pace of work.
