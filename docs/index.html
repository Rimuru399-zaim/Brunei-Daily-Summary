Brunei Urged to Preserve Fiscal Buffers and Accelerate Diversification

The ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office has advised Brunei Darussalam to safeguard fiscal reserves and expand non-oil industries to strengthen economic resilience.

By the Dispatch Desk

Brunei Darussalam must safeguard its fiscal buffers and step up economic diversification efforts to build resilience against external shocks, according to an assessment by the ASEAN+3 Macroeconomic Research Office following an annual consultation visit from June 10 to 18, 2026.

Although the nation maintains stable macroeconomic fundamentals supported by low inflation and a strong external position, its heavy reliance on the oil and gas sector continues to expose the economy to global commodity price volatility.

Near-term economic growth is expected to moderate to a more sustainable pace following recent rebounds in energy production, driven by structural factors linked to the hydrocarbon economy and broader global uncertainties.

The research office advised that prudent fiscal management, efficient public spending, and strengthened fiscal frameworks are essential to maintain buffers built primarily from hydrocarbon revenues.

Advancing diversification into non-oil sectors including services and manufacturing, alongside investments in human capital and productivity, remains central as the country pursues its Wawasan 2035 goals.

BDCB warns public against AI-generated investment scams

The Brunei Darussalam Central Bank has warned the public to remain vigilant against videos generated or manipulated using artificial intelligence to promote an investment scheme. The warning follows the circulation of a fraudulent video misusing the central bank name and impersonating a senior government official to falsely claim that an investment product complied with central bank standards. As the regulator of the financial sector in Brunei Darussalam, the central bank clarified that it does not offer financial products or services to the public, nor does it promote or endorse investment platforms.

The central bank advised individuals to deal exclusively with licensed or regulated financial entities and to avoid disclosing personal or banking information in response to suspicious content. Members of the public who encounter suspicious financial activities are encouraged to report them to the Financial Consumer Issues unit at the central bank, as well as to relevant social media platforms and the Brunei Computer Emergency Response Team. The bank noted that impersonation for cheating, disseminating false information that causes public alarm, and the unauthorized use of its name or logo are offences under existing laws.

Universiti Teknologi Brunei, through its Centre for Transport Research, hosted an international sharing session focused on smart mobility and sustainable cities at its library complex. Thailand approves tougher security measures, reviews peace dialogue

Thailand has approved enhanced security measures for its southern border provinces following a recent rise in violence. Announced by National Security Council Secretary-General Chatchai Bangchuad after a meeting chaired by Prime Minister Anutin Charnvirakul, the measures include stricter law enforcement, expanded surveillance, tighter border controls, and a review of the peace dialogue framework with the Barisan Revolusi Nasional. Military and police forces have been instructed to monitor cross-border movements to prevent suspects from fleeing to neighbouring countries.

The decision to reassess the peace process follows the temporary suspension of talks originally scheduled for September in Malaysia, which were put on hold after a July attack in Narathiwat killed five rangers. Although future talks depend on appropriate circumstances and a conducive atmosphere, Chatchai stressed that the government remains committed to the peace dialogue and its broader development policies for the southern border region.

AI scammers outperform humans when it comes to building trust

Researchers from four universities evaluated the potential of generative artificial intelligence chatbots within the scam industry, specifically focusing on text-based romance fraud that transitions into fake crypto investments. In a simulation comparing artificial intelligence chatbots to humans during the trust-building conversations preceding a fake investment request, the chatbots effectively impersonated humans.

During the relationship-establishing stages of the fraud process, which often stretch to months in real-world interactions, the study revealed that artificial intelligence chatbots performed remarkably effectively. By some measures, the chatbots even outperformed human scammers in establishing victim trust.

Max-severity Exchange server flaw under active exploitation by Kremlin hackers

Security researchers reported that Russian state hackers are leveraging a maximum-severity vulnerability in Microsoft Outlook Exchange Server to breach unpatched devices, stealing credentials and confidential data. The threat actor, identified by researchers at Proofpoint as TA488, acts on behalf of the Kremlin and was previously warned about alongside the National Security Agency for exploiting a Zimbra email vulnerability.

The attack mechanism requires no action from a user beyond opening an email sent to an Outlook Web Access account to install advanced malware. Proofpoint researchers noted that the infection chain deploys a previously unknown JavaScript browser-based implant named OWAReaper to maintain persistent access.
