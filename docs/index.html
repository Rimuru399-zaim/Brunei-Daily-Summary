ASEAN

S’pore weighs action against firms linked to haze

Singapore is examining companies that may be responsible for Indonesian forest fires worsening regional haze conditions, according to Prime Minister Lawrence Wong. Wong stated that the government would take action under Singapore's Transboundary Haze Pollution Act where warranted and is exploring joint action with other countries. The law permits authorities to prosecute and fine firms even when illegal land-clearing or burning occurs outside the country.

Wong stated that the haze originates from forest and peatland fires worsened by an extended dry period, noting that Singapore has offered assistance to Indonesia and will keep working with regional partners. While acknowledging that authorities cannot directly control events beyond national borders, he said the government would do everything possible to protect Singaporeans and ensure support is available.

Tuan Ibrahim denies defaming Anwar over royal dispute, corruption remarks

PAS deputy president Tuan Ibrahim Tuan Man has denied defaming Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, stating in a defence filed on Sept 15 that his remarks on the Negeri Sembilan royal succession dispute, police investigations, and corruption were based on facts and public events. Tuan Ibrahim maintained that Anwar interfered in the dispute by recognising Tuanku Muhriz Tuanku Munawir as Yang Dipertuan Besar following a proclamation by Tunku Nadzaruddin Tuanku Ja'afar, and he cited an earlier judgment regarding alleged misconduct and corruption.

In his reply, Anwar described the defence as "scraping the barrel" and a desperate effort to justify the remarks, arguing that an earlier judgment is not proof of misappropriation, corruption, or abuse of power. The suit stems from a speech delivered at a PAS ceramah in Felda Jenderak Utara, Kuala Krau, Pahang, on June 14, with Anwar seeking damages and an injunction.

Targeted ads delivered 0-click exploits to install mercenary malware

According to research published Thursday by the University of Toronto’s Citizen Lab, mercenary hackers placed zero-click exploits inside targeted advertisements to install malware on individuals' devices. The attacks infected iOS and Android devices without requiring user interaction when targets simply loaded a website or opened an application, leaving no visible trace unless detected by specialized forensic tools. The campaign, which Citizen Lab designated AdFection, was confirmed to have infected 45 devices at Al Jazeera during 2024 and 2025.

Citizen Lab researchers cautioned that the findings indicate newsrooms, reporters, editors, and other staff members globally could face active targeting through such methods. The report noted that circulating spyware through targeted advertising creates security dangers for individual victims, civil society, and states.
